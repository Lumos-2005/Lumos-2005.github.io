Featured Independent Software Project

Sound Control Lite

Native macOS Application · Swift · SwiftUI · CoreAudio

A packaged per-app audio mixer for macOS

I independently designed and built Sound Control Lite to solve a missing macOS control: changing one app's volume without altering the system volume. The menu-bar app provides per-app sliders, mute and reset controls, real-time audio activity detection, and a focused view of apps currently producing sound.

I developed the native SwiftUI interface and CoreAudio integration, packaged the app as a downloadable DMG, and iterated through public beta releases, including compatibility work for Zoom Share Sound routing.

Current Undergraduate Research

Single-cell aging and senescence

The research develops a computational workflow for single-cell RNA sequencing data—measurements of gene activity in individual cells—to determine whether gene-expression patterns can be organized into structured representations that help characterize or predict aging- and senescence-related cellular state. The central challenge is separating biologically meaningful structure from patterns that are specific to one dataset or measurement process.

Responsibilities span preparing and interpreting single-cell datasets, constructing reduced and interpretable representations of biological variation, and training or evaluating models that relate those representations to aging and senescence. Candidate approaches are compared with simpler baselines and subjected to statistical validation, controlled synthetic benchmarks, and failure analysis. Potential signals are audited for technical and biological confounding, then evaluated on independent datasets to test transfer.

Building this workflow required rapid cross-domain learning across single-cell biology, gene-expression analysis, computational biology, and machine learning. It applies ECE perspectives on measurement, noise, representation, systems, and quantitative validation to biological data. The research process is organized around hypothesis-driven experiments, reproducible experiment organization, baseline comparisons, iterative debugging, falsification, and explicit decisions about whether evidence supports a proposed direction.
